Who these teams are for

Players who are still building the things that make organized basketball make sense — and who deserve a real team environment while they do it.

  • Individual skills
  • Rules and game flow
  • Spacing
  • Team concepts
  • Decision-making
  • Basketball IQ
  • Confidence
  • Understanding organized basketball

How players get there

There are no formal open tryouts for standard Triumph development teams. Players usually arrive through training, where coaches can see them regularly.

AAU / travel teams

Established teams,
built on purpose.

Triumph currently operates approximately nine established competitive teams, roughly 3rd grade through high school, competing in weekend shootouts, tournaments and travel events depending on age and level.

Why rosters stay small

Competitive rosters are intentionally kept to approximately 8–9 players whenever appropriate. It isn't about promising minutes — it's about what a season looks like when a coach has nine players instead of fourteen.

  • More meaningful repetitions in practice and games
  • More individual coaching per player
  • More accountability — nobody hides
  • More opportunities to contribute and make decisions
  • A better environment for actual development

Playing time is earned, not guaranteed. Smaller rosters create more opportunity — they don't remove the standard.

Photo — Triumph competitive team

We don't build teams simply because enough players are interested.

We build teams when we have the right players, the right developmental environment, and the right coach to lead them. Teams are already established, so there may not always be an open roster spot at a given age.

If the appropriate roster is full: train with Triumph, stay connected, keep developing — and be considered when the right opportunity opens. Plenty of current Triumph players got there exactly that way.
